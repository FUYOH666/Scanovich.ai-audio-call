# Pilot Outreach Playbook

This document is for the first 2–5 serious product conversations.

The goal is not mass outreach. The goal is to find people with the exact pain the product already solves and to speak with them honestly.

## Who to contact first

Prioritize warm and adjacent contacts:

- former colleagues,
- operations leaders,
- QA managers,
- support leads,
- sales managers with call review burden,
- businesses where managers still listen to calls manually.

Do not start with cold mass messaging.

## What to offer

Offer a narrow pilot conversation, not a giant project:

- one team,
- one call type,
- 3 to 10 recordings,
- one success question,
- one short evaluation window.

Good examples:

- appointment-booking quality review,
- support-call QA for one queue,
- sales-call script compliance for one branch.

## One-paragraph outreach message

Use something this simple:

> I built a local-first tool that helps teams stop manually listening to full call recordings one by one. It transcribes calls, scores quality, and keeps the data on infrastructure you control. If your team still spends time reviewing calls manually, I would love to show a very small pilot on 3–10 recordings and see whether it saves time or surfaces useful coaching insights.

## First conversation structure

Keep the first conversation short and calm:

1. Ask how they review calls today.
2. Ask what part is slow, painful, or subjective.
3. Ask one success question:
   - would success mean faster QA,
   - better coaching,
   - better visibility,
   - or fewer missed bad calls?
4. Offer the smallest possible next step.

## What to avoid

Avoid:

- promising a platform,
- talking about future SaaS features,
- listing every planned integration,
- pushing for a contract before the fit is clear.

## Pilot note template

After each conversation, write down:

- company or team,
- contact person,
- current workflow,
- pain in their own words,
- narrow use case,
- main objection,
- next action,
- whether they sound like a real pilot candidate.

## Green flags

Strong pilot signs:

- they already review calls manually,
- the process is painful and repetitive,
- privacy matters,
- one team can own the pilot,
- they can provide a few representative recordings quickly.

## Red flags

Be careful when:

- they want instant full SaaS onboarding,
- they need diarization immediately,
- they want multi-tenant platform behaviour from day one,
- nobody on their side owns the evaluation.

## Recommended cadence

For a solo builder, a good starting target is:

- 10 warm contacts,
- 3 meaningful conversations,
- 1 strong follow-up pilot candidate.

That is enough to produce real signal without burning yourself out.
